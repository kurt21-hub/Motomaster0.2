Introduction
Get an overview of battery maintenance — why it is critical for vehicle reliability and what this module covers.
Estimated time: 3 min
Difficulty: Easy
What You Will Learn

Understand the role of the battery and signs of failure.
2

3
Follow an 11-step procedure to safely replace a vehicle battery.
LESSON 2 OF 15
Tools & Materials
Prepare all required tools and safety equipment before starting battery maintenance.
Estimated time: 3 min
Difficulty: Easy
Required Tools
1
Replacement battery (matching CCA rating and size)
2
Wrench set (typically 10 mm)
3
Wire brush or battery terminal cleaner
4
Baking soda and water solution (for corrosion)
5
Safety glasses and acid-resistant gloves
Safety Reminder
Batteries contain sulfuric acid and produce hydrogen gas. Always wear eye protection and gloves, and work in a ventilated area away from sparks.
LESSON 3 OF 15 — STEP 1
Safety Precautions
Review critical safety rules before working on the battery.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Turn off the ignition and remove the key.
2
Put on safety glasses and acid-resistant gloves before touching the battery.
3
Ensure no open flames or smoking near the battery — hydrogen gas can ignite.
Safety Reminder
Do not place metal tools across the battery terminals — this can cause a dangerous short circuit and arc flash.
LESSON 4 OF 15 — STEP 2
Locate the Battery
Find the battery in the engine bay or trunk depending on the vehicle model.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Open the hood and look for a rectangular plastic case with two cable terminals (red and black).
2
On some vehicles the battery is in the trunk or under a rear seat — check the owner's manual.
LESSON 5 OF 15 — STEP 3
Inspect the Battery
Assess the battery condition before deciding whether to clean or replace it.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Check the terminals for white or blue corrosion buildup.
2
Look for a swollen or cracked case — this indicates the battery must be replaced.
3
Check the manufacture date label; batteries older than 3–5 years are nearing end of life.
LESSON 6 OF 15 — STEP 4
Disconnect the Negative Cable
Always remove the negative (–) cable first to prevent short circuits.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Identify the black cable connected to the terminal marked "–" or "NEG".
2
Use a 10 mm wrench to loosen the clamp nut.
3
Wiggle the clamp free and move the cable away from the battery.
Safety Reminder
Negative first, positive last — this is the universal rule for battery disconnection to prevent sparks.
LESSON 7 OF 15 — STEP 5
Disconnect the Positive Cable
Remove the positive (+) cable after the negative is safely set aside.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Identify the red cable connected to the terminal marked "+" or "POS".
2
Loosen the clamp nut with a wrench and remove the cable from the terminal.
3
Keep the positive cable away from any metal surfaces.
LESSON 8 OF 15 — STEP 6
Remove the Battery
Unbolt the battery hold-down bracket and carefully lift the battery out.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Remove the hold-down bracket bolt or clamp at the base of the battery.
2
Lift the battery straight up — keep it level to avoid spilling acid.
3
Set it on a flat, stable surface away from the vehicle.
Safety Reminder
Car batteries are heavy (15–25 kg). Lift with your legs, not your back. Do not tilt — acid may leak from vent caps.
LESSON 9 OF 15 — STEP 7
Clean the Battery Tray & Terminals
Remove corrosion from the battery tray and cable terminals before installing the new battery.
5 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Mix one tablespoon of baking soda with one cup of water.
2
Apply the solution to corroded areas on the tray and clamps, then scrub with a wire brush.
3
Rinse with clean water and dry thoroughly with a rag.
LESSON 10 OF 15 — STEP 8
Install the New Battery
Place the new battery into the tray with the correct terminal orientation.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Lower the new battery into the tray, matching the positive terminal to the positive cable side.
2
Reinstall the hold-down bracket and tighten securely — the battery must not move.
Safety Reminder
A loose battery can shift during driving and cause terminal shorts or damage to the engine bay.
LESSON 11 OF 15 — STEP 9
Connect the Positive Cable
Reconnect the positive (+) cable first when installing a battery.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Slide the red cable clamp onto the positive (+) terminal.
2
Tighten the clamp nut firmly with a wrench — the cable should not rotate on the post.
LESSON 12 OF 15 — STEP 10
Connect the Negative Cable
Reconnect the negative (–) cable last to complete the circuit safely.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Slide the black cable clamp onto the negative (–) terminal.
2
Tighten the clamp nut securely.
3
Apply a thin coat of terminal grease or petroleum jelly to both terminals to prevent future corrosion.
Safety Reminder
Positive first, negative last — this is the universal rule for reconnection to prevent sparks.
LESSON 13 OF 15 — STEP 11
Test and Verify
Start the engine and verify the battery and electrical system are working correctly.
5 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Start the engine and verify it cranks smoothly without hesitation.
2
Check that all dashboard warning lights extinguish normally after startup.
3
Test electrical components — headlights, wipers, and radio — to confirm full power.

LESSON 15 OF 15
Summary
Review the key takeaways from the Battery Maintenance module.
Key Takeaways
1
Always remove negative first, reconnect negative last — this prevents dangerous sparks.
2
Clean corrosion with baking soda solution and a wire brush before fitting the new battery.
3
Secure the hold-down bracket firmly — a loose battery is a safety hazard.
4
Apply terminal grease to prevent future corrosion and ensure good electrical contact.
