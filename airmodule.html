LESSON 1 OF 12
Introduction
Get an overview of what you will learn — the complete air filter replacement process from inspection to reinstallation.
Estimated time: 3 min
Difficulty: Easy
What You Will Learn
1
Understand why a clean air filter is critical for engine performance and fuel efficiency.
2
Identify the tools and materials needed for an air filter replacement.
3
Follow an 8-step procedure to safely replace the engine air filter.
LESSON 2 OF 12
Tools & Materials
Gather all necessary tools and materials before starting the air filter replacement.
Estimated time: 3 min
Difficulty: Easy
Required Tools
1
New replacement air filter (check vehicle manual for correct size)
2
Flathead screwdriver or socket set (for housing clips/bolts)
3
Clean cloth or shop rag
4
Compressed air or vacuum (optional, for cleaning housing)
Safety Reminder
Always work on a cool engine. Never replace an air filter immediately after driving.
LESSON 3 OF 12 — STEP 1
Locate the Air Filter Housing
Find the air filter housing in the engine bay before starting any work.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Open the vehicle hood and secure it with the prop rod.
2
Locate the air filter housing — it is typically a large black plastic box connected to a large rubber intake hose.
Safety Reminder
Ensure the engine is off and fully cooled before reaching into the engine bay.
LESSON 4 OF 12 — STEP 2
Inspect the Old Filter
Before removing, assess the condition of the existing air filter.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Unclip one side of the housing lid to peek at the filter.
2
Hold the filter up to the light — if you cannot see light through the folds, it must be replaced.
3
Check for oil contamination, insects, or physical damage.
LESSON 5 OF 12 — STEP 3
Disconnect the Air Intake Hose
Loosen the clamp securing the rubber intake hose to the housing.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Use a flathead screwdriver or socket to loosen the hose clamp screw.
2
Gently twist and pull the rubber hose away from the housing port.
Safety Reminder
Do not force or kink the intake hose — it can crack and cause an air leak affecting engine performance.
LESSON 6 OF 12 — STEP 4
Open the Filter Housing
Unclip or unscrew the air filter housing lid to access the filter inside.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Release all spring clips or unscrew the bolts holding the housing lid in place.
2
Lift the lid away carefully and set it aside without straining any attached sensor wires.
LESSON 7 OF 12 — STEP 5
Remove the Old Air Filter
Lift the old filter out of the housing without shaking debris into the intake.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Carefully lift the old filter straight up out of the housing.
2
Avoid tilting or shaking it — loose debris can fall into the intake manifold.
3
Place the old filter in a bag immediately for disposal.
Safety Reminder
Cover the open intake port with a clean cloth while the housing is open to prevent debris ingestion.
LESSON 8 OF 12 — STEP 6
Clean the Housing
Remove any dirt, dust, or debris from inside the air filter housing before fitting the new filter.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Use a clean dry cloth to wipe out the interior of the housing.
2
If available, use compressed air or a vacuum to remove fine dust particles.
3
Inspect the housing for cracks or damage that may cause air leaks.
LESSON 9 OF 12 — STEP 7
Install the New Air Filter
Place the new filter correctly into the housing with the rubber seal facing down.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Verify the new filter matches the old one in size and shape.
2
Lower the new filter into the housing, ensuring the rubber gasket seats evenly all the way around.
3
Press down gently to confirm a snug, even fit.
LESSON 10 OF 12 — STEP 8
Close and Secure the Housing
Reattach the housing lid and reconnect the intake hose to complete the installation.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Place the housing lid back on and fasten all clips or bolts firmly.
2
Reattach the rubber intake hose to the housing port and tighten the hose clamp.
3
Start the engine and listen for any unusual whistling or air-sucking sounds indicating a leak.
Safety Reminder
Never run the engine with the air filter housing open — unfiltered air can damage the engine internals.

LESSON 12 OF 12
Summary
Review the key takeaways from the Air Filter Replacement module.
Key Takeaways
1
A clogged air filter reduces engine power and fuel efficiency — replace every 15,000–30,000 km.
2
Always cover the open intake port when the housing is removed.
3
Ensure the filter gasket seats evenly to prevent unfiltered air bypass.
4
Test the engine after reassembly and listen for air leaks.
