LESSON 2 OF 14
Tools & Materials
Gather all necessary tools and materials before beginning the spark plug replacement.
Estimated time: 3 min
Difficulty: Easy
Required Tools
1
New spark plugs (matching OEM specification)
2
Spark plug socket (typically 5/8" or 16 mm) with extension bar
3
Torque wrench
4
Spark plug gap gauge
5
Anti-seize compound and dielectric grease
Safety Reminder
Always work on a fully cooled engine. Hot spark plug threads can cross-thread or cause burns.
LESSON 3 OF 14 — STEP 1
Let the Engine Cool
Allow sufficient cooling time before touching any engine components.
30 min wait
Easy
Instructions
1
Turn off the engine and open the hood.
2
Wait at least 30 minutes for the engine to fully cool before proceeding.
Safety Reminder
Installing spark plugs into a hot engine can cause thread damage or aluminum head warping — never rush this step.
LESSON 4 OF 14 — STEP 2
Locate the Spark Plugs
Find each spark plug on the engine — the number matches the cylinder count.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Follow the ignition wires or coil-on-plug connectors leading from the ignition system to the engine head.
2
Each wire or coil corresponds to one spark plug — work one at a time.
LESSON 5 OF 14 — STEP 3
Remove the Ignition Wire or Coil
Disconnect the ignition wire or coil-on-plug from the first spark plug.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
For ignition wire: grip the rubber boot — not the wire — and twist while pulling straight out.
2
For coil-on-plug: unclip the electrical connector, then remove the retaining bolt and lift the coil straight up.
Safety Reminder
Replace plugs one at a time to avoid mixing up the firing order — label wires if necessary.
LESSON 6 OF 14 — STEP 4
Remove the Spark Plug
Use the spark plug socket to unscrew and extract the old plug.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Insert the spark plug socket with extension bar into the plug well.
2
Turn counter-clockwise to loosen, then remove the plug by hand for the final turns.
3
Keep the plug upright to avoid debris falling into the cylinder.
LESSON 7 OF 14 — STEP 5
Inspect the Old Spark Plug
Read the condition of the old plug to identify any engine issues.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Light tan or grey deposit — normal wear, replacement is due.
2
Black sooty deposit — rich mixture or oil fouling, investigate further.
3
Melted or blistered electrode — engine overheating or incorrect heat range.
LESSON 8 OF 14 — STEP 6
Check the Gap
Verify the new spark plug gap matches the vehicle's specification before installation.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Slide the correct feeler gauge blade between the center and ground electrode.
2
The gauge should pass with slight resistance — check the spec in your owner's manual (typically 0.8–1.1 mm).
3
Bend the ground electrode gently with a gap tool to adjust if needed.
Safety Reminder
An incorrect gap causes misfires, poor fuel economy, and hard starting — always verify before installing.
LESSON 9 OF 14 — STEP 7
Apply Anti-Seize Compound
Coat the plug threads lightly with anti-seize to ease future removal.
2 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Apply a very thin coat of anti-seize compound to the first two or three threads only.
2
Do NOT apply anti-seize to iridium or platinum plugs — check the plug manufacturer's recommendation first.
LESSON 10 OF 14 — STEP 8
Install the New Spark Plug
Thread the new plug into the cylinder head carefully by hand first.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Place the new plug into the socket and lower it carefully into the plug well.
2
Thread in by hand (clockwise) until finger-tight — this prevents cross-threading.
Safety Reminder
Never use the ratchet to start threading — cross-threading an aluminum head is a costly mistake.
LESSON 11 OF 14 — STEP 9
Torque to Specification
Use a torque wrench to tighten the plug to the manufacturer's specified torque.
3 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Set the torque wrench to the specified value (typically 15–25 Nm — confirm in vehicle manual).
2
Tighten until the torque wrench clicks — do not over-tighten.
Safety Reminder
Over-tightening can crack the porcelain insulator or strip the threads. Always use a torque wrench.
LESSON 12 OF 14 — STEP 10
Reattach Wire or Coil & Test
Reconnect the ignition wire or coil-on-plug and start the engine to verify operation.
5 min
Easy
Instructions
1
Apply dielectric grease inside the boot, then press the wire boot or coil firmly onto the plug until it clicks.
2
Repeat Steps 3–10 for each remaining spark plug.
3
Start the engine — listen for smooth idling with no misfires or rough running.

LESSON 14 OF 14
Summary
Review the key takeaways from the Spark Plug Replacement module.
Key Takeaways
1
Always work on a cool engine — never install plugs into a hot head.
2
Replace plugs one at a time to maintain the correct firing order.
3
Verify the gap before installing and torque to specification with a torque wrench.
4
Inspect the old plug condition — it can reveal underlying engine problems.
